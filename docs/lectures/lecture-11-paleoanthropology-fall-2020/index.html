Slide 14

  • Relative Dating : Chemical Tests
  • Nitrogen Test
  • Nitrogen-containing amino acids break down as the bone fossilizes
  • Amount of Nitrogen indicates relative age
  • Fluorine Test
  • Bones absorb Fluorine from surrounding groundwater
  • Combines with calcium
  • Amount of Fluoroapatite indicates relative age

Slide 16

  • Chronometric Dating : Radiometric
  • Radioactive atoms are unstable and decay, accumulating at a regular rate
  • Parent atoms decay to daughter atoms
  • Time determined by ratio of daughter to parent atoms
  • Some action sets the “clock” to zero
  • Burial in the earth
  • Superheating associated with volcanic activity
  • http://www.bible.ca/tracks/dating-radiometric.htm

Slide 24

  • Fission-Track
  • Measures side effects of fission of 238U
  • Split of nucleus leaves damage tracks on rock crystals
  • Counts the tracks
  • A function of age, U present
  • Tracks can fade over time and at elevated temperatures

Slide 25

  • Electronic Trap Techniques
  • Chronometric Dating
  • Don’t rely on radioactive decay
  • Measure accumulation of electrons trapped in crystal lattice of specimen

Slide 26

  • Thermoluminescence
  • Electrons ‘escape,’ but are trapped in the crystal lattice of the material
  • Heating returns them to where they started
  • Light emitted by thermal activation is a measure of the electrons released and therefore the age

Slide 27

  • Electronic Spin Resonance
  • Also checks for unstable electrons
  • Measures the absorption of microwave radiation
  • Tooth Enamel, calcite in cave deposits, fossil shells, and coral
  • Enamel starts to record radioactivity in environment after death
  • A few thousand years to 300,000 years

Slide 28

  • Amino Acid Dating (Racemization)
  • Protein molecules in living things begin to decay at death
  • L-amino acids convert to D-amino acids
  • Temperature dependent
