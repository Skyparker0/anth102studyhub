Slide 8

  • Estimation of Time Since Death
  • Access by insects
  • Stages of insect development
  • Blowflies immediately after death
  • Deposit eggs on orifices
  • Insect successions
  • Carrion beetles after 2 days
Notes:

Age of larvae used to estimate age since death Seasonal insects can give clues to season of death, as insect deposition occurs soon after death. Blowflies lay eggs on body immediately. Eggs take 6-40 hours to hatch. In 3 to 10 days, maggots migrate off of body and bury in ground nearby to pupate. They return to the body after about day 18. Eventually beetles will consume flesh and then flies, and eventually outnumber flies. Ratio can be used to estimate time since death. Insects can only be used between 1 day and 1 year.

  • Bone Changes
  • Loss of trabecular bone
  • Osteon counts in cortical bone
  • Increase with age
  • Change in structure to adapt to stresses
Notes:

After bone growth ceases (modeling), a remodeling phase begins. This is only visible microscopically. During some of this remodeling, some bone is removed, leaving holes (Haversian canals), which is replaced by secondary lamellar bone. Resultant structure looks like a bulls eye, called a Haversian canal. These can be counted.
